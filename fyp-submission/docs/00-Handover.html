Do these things before you submit

  1. Open Project_Thesis.docx in Word, click into the Table of Contents, and press F9 → "Update entire table." No script can compute real page numbers — that only happens inside Word, once, by you.
  2. Read 05-Test-Cases.md's banner at the very top before you write or say anything about testing in your report or viva. It states plainly how many test cases were actually executed vs. only specified. Do not round that number up.
  3. Sign the certificate-of-approval and declaration pages in the thesis front matter — they have real signature-line blocks, left blank intentionally.
  4. Check your institution's exact required wording on the certificate/declaration pages against what's here — this was written from the standard FYP submission form, not a copy of a signed institutional template.
  5. Read 05-Test-Cases.md §6, the defect log, in full. Two of the fifteen logged defects are still open (MFA is unimplemented; the admin panel is incomplete) — know these before a panel member finds them for you.
  6. Rehearse 01-Simple-Guide.md §9's viva questions out loud, especially the ones about the scam classifier's real accuracy and the fact that nothing is deployed publicly yet. Panels ask the uncomfortable question; a fluent honest answer defends better than a confident evasive one.

Do NOT do these things

  • Do not hand-edit any generated file (docs/pdf/*.pdf, docs/diagrams/*.png) — they're regenerated from the Markdown sources. Edit the .md file and rebuild.
  • Do not submit the browser-print PDF of the thesis. pdf/07-Thesis.pdf is a reading/email convenience copy. The actual submission copy is Project_Thesis.docx, exported to PDF from Word after you've updated the Table of Contents.
  • Do not present "51 test cases specified" as "51 test cases passed." They are different numbers, stated separately on purpose in 05-Test-Cases.md.
  • Do not claim the system is deployed publicly. It is not, as of this document. Say so plainly if asked — the deployment plan is designed and partly rehearsed, not live.
  • Do not claim validated real-world OCR/face-match accuracy. No consented real CNIC/selfie test set has been collected yet — that's still an open item, and it's yours to close (see below).

Honest status, without spin

The software is genuinely built and working end-to-end for the core journey: a seller can register, verify email, sign in, complete identity/phone/email/device checks, see an explainable Trust Score with a full breakdown, appeal it, have a reviewer decide, and see it recompute. A buyer can look up that score with no account. The engineering is solid — 330 automated tests pass, static analysis is clean, and the codebase follows a deliberately layered, testable architecture. Two real gaps remain open and are documented plainly rather than hidden: the admin panel is functionally incomplete (only the review queue and scoring-model tuning work end-to-end), and MFA for staff accounts is designed but not implemented. We have not independently validated OCR/face-match accuracy against real documents, and the custom scam classifier is measurably weak on real target-domain text (1 of 7 real scam ads caught in a manual check) — which is exactly why it is not yet wired into live scoring. None of this was hidden while writing this package; all of it is written up in 05-Test-Cases.md and the SDD's honest-limitations sections, because a report that states its real gaps plainly is more credible to a supervisor than one that doesn't have any.

One more thing found live while building this package, worth knowing before your viva: simply signing in and browsing between the three demo seller accounts silently overwrites their intended LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH score spread with a uniform capped HIGH score, because a routine device-fingerprint check triggers a real score recomputation and the demo seed never stored a selfie file for any of them. It's logged as DEF-14 in 05-Test-Cases.md §6 and is not yet fixed — rehearse your demo account switching carefully, or you may find all three sellers showing the same score live in front of the panel. The scores were restored for this package's own screenshots; they will corrupt again the next time someone clicks through all three accounts unless DEF-14 is actually fixed.

What's still yours to do, per REMAINING.md Track D in the project root: collect the real CNIC and face test images (this is explicitly a student task, not an engineering one — see REMAINING.md Stage 1), rehearse the architecture walkthrough and viva defence, and write and present the final deck. This documentation package supplies the technical content, diagrams, and measured numbers for your report; you write and defend it, because you're the ones at the viva.
